# Deconstruct the reference

Inspect the reference, brief and canonical plan. Present the intended compositions, selected actions, and the independent parts/backing they need. Use `plan spec inspect/check/apply` for intent and [image-to-layer planning](../../../docs/workflows/01-reference-and-layers.md) for spatial reasoning.

Survey focal point, palette, painted edges, light sources, depth, perspective and meaningful object families before choosing the first cutouts. Distinguish observed details from proposed ones. A still image does not establish unseen geometry or an accurate depth map. Follow [seed-to-stage](../../../docs/SEED-TO-STAGE.md) and [style guidance](../../../docs/STYLE-GUIDE.md) within the user's scope.

For each selected action, state its cause or thematic contribution, the cues establishing it, and the appropriate emphasis. Use existing element purpose, action description and expectation direction; text alone does not prove an artistic choice. Deliberate stillness remains valid. Do not invent a draft or haunting merely to explain already-produced movement; see [motion direction](../../../docs/MOTION-DIRECTION.md).

Plan requested portrait/landscape compositions together: subject size, crop exclusions, source resolution, overlap and maximum reveal. The seed crop is not automatically the production frame. Boundaries follow intended actions and requested exploration, including independent moving parts, fixed occluders, contacts and shadows. A cropped composite cannot supply independent controls or missing hidden paint.

Keep semantic intent in the [production plan](../../../docs/PRODUCTION-PLAN.md), fulfillment in inventory, and detailed spatial decisions in layer/layout notes referring to the same IDs. Do not duplicate editable censuses. Plan pixels behind moving objects and beyond frame edges; choose camera limits before overscan.

Use cels for internal deformation, transforms for rigid motion, and stable mounting for architecture unless structural change is intentional. Present small paired blocking proofs before detailed new-film art, or a focused layer proof for a revision. Intent/layout evidence includes preserved reference, brief, backing/occlusion and attachment decisions, plus explicit observations. A plan does not establish that art or safe motion exists.
