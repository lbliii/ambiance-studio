# Painted ambiance: art and motion guide

## The artistic promise

A place that feels inhabited, inviting, and a little mysterious. The viewer should want to remain there. The painting must work as a still; motion reveals its life rather than competing with the composition.

These are the defaults learned from The Last Lantern. The user's reference and explicit direction take precedence. A snowy café should not inherit Halloween pumpkins merely because the first library contains them.

## Shape, color, and depth

Use readable silhouettes, designed shadow shapes, and painterly color variation within those shapes. Let selected contours carry the cel character; outlining every small detail makes the image brittle. Keep architecture and perspective stable across cels.

Separate distance with contrast, saturation, edge sharpness, overlap, and scale before relying on camera movement. In the reference, the distant town sits in cool atmospheric color, the cottage carries the warm focal light, and dark foreground branches frame the view. Preserve that hierarchy when adapting a new palette.

Choose a small set of light roles: key source, warm practical lights, cool environmental fill, and restrained emissive accents. A window glow should influence a nearby surface enough to feel connected. Leave room in the base painting for light to change; a fully bright painted window cannot visibly brighten much further.

Keep the main visual invitation readable at phone size. Reserve detail for places the viewer can discover over repeated watching. A few coherent details are more effective than uniformly dense texture.

## Layer design

Plan a spatial stack, such as sky → distant atmosphere → hills/town → woods → focal building → ground → near framing. This is a starting structure, not a required layer count. Separate an object only when it needs different movement, occlusion, lighting, or reuse.

Every separation has a cost: it may need a matte, clean backing, shadow, attachment, or edge repair. A transparent cutout alone does not supply the artwork behind it. Restrict camera movement to what the painting can support.

Maintain a style reference for each pack: palette, edge treatment, light direction, perspective, texture scale, and output scale. Reject an otherwise attractive asset when those relationships make it look pasted in.

## Motion language

Choose one main motion idea, supporting life, and a quiet background. A plume can change shape, a curtain can shift, a branch can sway, and clouds can pass the moon; they should not all compete for attention at once.

Use actual changing drawings for deformation and internal detail. Use transforms for rigid motion. Combine an animated cloud's cels with slow translation when both help. Stable cottage structure with animated windows is a good modular pattern.

Low cel rates can provide nostalgic stepping while a 30 fps camera remains smooth. The successful example used two new cloud cels per second and six smoke cels per second. These are reference choices, not universal targets. Review the effect at actual display size and speed.

Use compatible but different periods and phase offsets to avoid synchronized pulsing. Preserve the loop mathematically. Avoid noticeable resets, repeated unique gestures every few seconds, or large camera moves that make a quiet scene restless.

## Sound language

Give the scene an emotional sentence. Describe where the listener stands and what can be heard from there. Musical identity, near shelter, outside air, and one distant event can establish a world without filling every second.

Nostalgia need not sound damaged. Start with clean sources and intentionally choose timbre, melody, spacing, and acoustic distance. Keep sharp events rounded and infrequent when the goal is restful. The reference's longer music/event cycle made the repeated picture feel less repetitive.

No single numerical loudness target defines this style. Preserve comfortable low-volume listening and enough headroom for soft detail. Check mono and a real phone speaker; headphones alone can conceal a mix that loses its focal sound on a small device.

## Review questions

- Can I name the focal point immediately at phone size?
- Does foreground, middle distance, and background read before playback?
- Does the movement deepen the feeling of place?
- Do outlines, lighting, texture, and perspective agree across assets?
- Can I notice a loop restart, drifting attachment, or repeated sound too easily?
- Would I willingly watch and listen for another three repetitions?

Save the answer with examples, timestamps, or layer names. A numerical “quality score” without those observations is not useful direction.
